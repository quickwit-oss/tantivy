  • Size. On large segments, Vortex and the tantivy columnar store full numeric and ordinal columns at the same size: both end up bitpacking the same values. Vortex is smaller when it finds structure that the tantivy column indexes do not exploit (missing values, regular sparse positions, near-trivial list offsets), and it is larger on small segments, because every Vortex file carries about 2 kB of fixed cost. Storing text natively in Vortex instead of ordinals plus tantivy's sorted dictionary is 1.3 to 2.7 times larger on high-cardinality fields.
  • Scan speed. Vortex decompresses chunks of ~80k values with vectorized kernels. On full columns that is ×1.4 to ×2.8 slower than tantivy's direct bit-unpacking in isolation, which becomes about 10 % once diluted in the aggregation work. On optional and multivalued columns Vortex is faster, because tantivy pays a doc-to-row translation per block.
  • The abstraction costs more than the storage. A registered ValueSource loses the aggregation fast paths that require the physical column. That costs ×1.56 on average on the full input, up to ×9.4, more than the Vortex storage itself. Letting two fast paths use bounds() instead of as_column() brings it down to ×1.20 on full and about ×1.00 elsewhere.
  • Selective queries are the hard case. Vortex is designed for scans: decompressing a chunk to read a handful of values makes the cost proportional to the column size, and the generic take() has too much per-call overhead for blocks of 64 docs. A point-access reader that compiles each compressed chunk once into a small accessor tree, reads batches of values straight from the compressed buffers, and promotes a chunk to full decompression once it turns out to be read densely, brings selective queries close to the tantivy columns: ×1.79 → ×1.42 on full, ×2.80 → ×1.22 on dense, ×3.77 → ×1.11 on multivalue. Sparse data stays around ×1.5, and floats and dates remain the slowest case.
  • Final numbers. Against the plain tantivy columnar (abstraction cost included), the final Vortex reader is at ×1.76 on full, ×1.11 on dense, ×1.14 on sparse and ×0.89 on multivalue (geometric means over all requests). With the bounds() patch, the abstraction share of these numbers mostly disappears.
  • Correctness. Vortex returns exactly the same results as the tantivy columns read through the same path, on every request and input.

Bottom line. On this benchmark, Vortex is not a decisive size win on merged segments and is a loss on small ones. It is competitive on scans, better on optional and multivalued columns, and needs a dedicated point-access path for search-style selective queries. The cheapest and most broadly useful improvement, whatever the storage, is to make tantivy's aggregation fast paths independent of the physical column.
