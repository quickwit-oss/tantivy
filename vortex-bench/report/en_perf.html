7. Performance

7.1 Methodology

The benchmark is a copy of benches/agg_bench.rs with the same requests and data. For every request, the three variants (columnar, opaque, vortex) are registered in the same binggan group, so they are interleaved: 32 group iterations, an auto-detected number of calls per sample, median reported. Interleaving matters on this machine: the absolute time of the columnar variant drifts by up to 7 % from one run to the next, but the ratios between variants measured within a run are stable. All ratios in this report are computed within a run, then the median over runs is taken.

Before each group, the request runs once per variant and its result is compared with the columnar result (section 9). Requests rejected by registered sources (multi_terms, composite, top_hits, missing on text: 20 of the 79 requests per input) only appear in the columnar variant.

Ratios are summarized with geometric means. A ratio below 1 means the variant is faster than the reference.

7.3 Decoding alone

To separate storage from aggregation logic, a micro-benchmark calls ValueSource::load_block directly, in blocks of 64 docs like the collector, on every doc ("scan") or on the ~10,000 docs matching the 1 % filter ("filtered"). Each measurement is the best of 15 repetitions, with a fresh source each time (caches shared across queries, such as point accessors, are kept, as they would be in production).

Scan: vortex / tantivy
Scan: nanoseconds per value (full input)
  • Full columns: tantivy reads a value in 0.17–0.43 ns, Vortex in 0.36–1.03 ns (×1.4 to ×2.8). Vortex decompresses an ~80k-row chunk into a canonical buffer, then the source copies the requested values and maps them to u64; tantivy bit-unpacks 64 values straight into the output block. The most expensive Vortex columns are score_f64 (ALP-RD recombines two parts and applies patches) and the dates (DateTimeParts recombines three parts).
  • Optional and multivalued columns: Vortex is faster (×0.5 to ×0.98). For every block, tantivy translates doc ids into row ids through its optional index (a rank query per doc) or its multivalue index (two offset reads per doc), then gathers the values. Vortex already has the validity bitmap and the list offsets decompressed with the chunk.
Filtered (1 %): tantivy vs the three Vortex access strategies

Decompressing whole chunks costs one to two orders of magnitude more than tantivy's direct access; Vortex's generic take() is barely better, and sometimes worse; point access brings full bitpacked columns within ×2.6–×3.6 of tantivy, dense columns within ×1.2, multivalued columns within ×1.2–×1.4, and makes Vortex faster than tantivy on sparse columns (×0.7–×0.86), where tantivy's sparse optional index is relatively expensive to query. Floats and dates remain the hardest case (×1.6 to ×7.6, except on the sparse input).

7.4 The abstraction penalty and the bounds() patch

The opaque variant reads exactly the same bytes through exactly the same codec as the columnar variant; the only difference is that the aggregation code does not see the physical column. On the full input this costs ×1.56 on average and up to ×9.4. The worst cases match the fast paths listed in section 2.4:

  • terms_status_with_date_histogram* and terms_status_with_histogram (×3.6 to ×9.4): the flattened terms × histogram collector needs the raw ColumnValues of two full columns;
  • cardinality_agg_low_card (×3.5): the pre-sized ordinal bitset needs the maximum ordinal;
  • terms_7, terms_zipf_90, terms_all_unique (×2.6 to ×2.9): the dense bucket storages are chosen from the maximum ordinal.

On the other inputs the penalty is smaller (×1.1 to ×1.2) because several of these fast paths require a full column anyway.

Two of these fast paths only read the column maximum, which ValueSource::bounds() already provides, with exactly the same value for physical columns, and whose contract ("global value bounds, for fast paths that need to size something up front") covers the use. The patch replaces those two reads. The tantivy aggregation tests (328) pass, the columnar variant does not move (geometric mean of times ×1.00 between runs), and the penalty mostly disappears:

opaque / columnar with the bounds() patch

What remains is the flattened terms × histogram collector (×2.3 to ×7.0 on full) and the one-doc stats shortcut under high-cardinality terms (×1.3 to ×1.4 on dense). With the patch, vortex / columnar becomes ×1.40 on full, ×1.00 on dense, ×1.05 on sparse and ×0.85 on multivalue (chunk-decode version).

7.5 The cost of the storage on scans

vortex / opaque, chunk-decode version (v2), all groups

Outside selective queries, the storage cost of Vortex is small or negative: ×1.09 on full, ×0.89 on dense, ×1.00 on sparse and ×0.75 on multivalue (geometric means without the filtered group). The decode micro-benchmark explains the pattern: Vortex pays a bulk-decompression and copy cost on full columns that is diluted by the aggregation work, and saves the doc-to-row translation on optional and multivalued columns. On the multivalue input, more than half of the requests run 25 % to 42 % faster with Vortex than with the opaque tantivy columns.

7.6 Selective queries, version by version

v1 kept a single decompressed chunk. Pure scans do not care, but sub-aggregations receive their docs in buffered batches grouped by parent bucket. On the sparse input a batch covers about 43,000 rows, often two chunks, and every bucket of the batch decompressed these chunks again: the sparse average was ×2.87 and some selective requests were 195 times slower than opaque. v2 keeps 8 chunks in an LRU and fixes it.

v2 still decompresses every chunk a selective query touches, which is every chunk. v3 adds point access (section 8):

The first point-access version (v3a) read every short run point by point. It fixed selective queries but made some scans slower: under a high-cardinality terms aggregation, a sub-aggregation over the whole segment receives a myriad of runs of one or two docs. v2 decompressed each chunk once and served everything from the LRU; v3a read every value point by point, which is more expensive, especially for ALP-RD floats (terms_many_with_avg_sub_agg went from ×1.14 to ×3.48). The final version adds a promotion rule (a "ski rental" strategy): point reads into a chunk are charged a cost per value plus a cost per run, and once the accumulated cost exceeds a fraction of the cost of decompressing the chunk, the chunk is decompressed and served from the LRU. Whatever the access pattern, the total stays within a small factor of the better of the two strategies.

The constants of the rule matter. A targeted comparison on the requests most sensitive to them:

A higher per-run cost promotes chunks earlier: it recovers the small loss on sparse sub-aggregations, but promotes chunks of selective queries too, which brings back part of the v2 cost on the filtered nested requests. The default (16) is the best compromise on this set. A cost model per encoding (point reads into a sparse chunk are relatively expensive, decompressing it is cheap) would remove most of the trade-off.

7.7 Many segments

On the 100- and 1,000-segment indexes, vortex / opaque stays at ×1.02 to ×1.05 in every version. Once the per-segment cache is warm, opening a segment costs nothing, and these requests are dominated by merging intermediate results across segments. The abstraction penalty (×1.2 to ×1.3) disappears with the bounds() patch.

7.8 Memory

The LRU of decompressed chunks costs up to about 5 MB per column and per collector for 64-bit columns (8 chunks of ~80k values). It is visible in binggan's memory measurement: average_u64 goes from a few kB to 4.9 MB. A pure scan would only need one chunk; the LRU size could depend on whether the collector is a sub-aggregation. Point accessors are shared per segment and reference the compressed buffers without copying them; they only materialize validity bitsets, patches and small dictionaries.

7.10 Stability

The chunk-decode version (v2) was measured in three full runs plus a targeted rerun of the 26 most extreme requests: over the 250 requests measured in all three variants, the median spread of the vortex / columnar ratio across runs is 1.2 %, the 90th percentile 3.7 %, the maximum 13 %. The extreme ratios of the selective group reproduced within ±2 %.

The final version (v3) was measured in two full runs, plus a third run of the four requests whose spread exceeded 10 %: median spread 0.6 %, 90th percentile 2.9 %. Those four (cardinality_agg_low_card, terms_all_unique and terms_many_with_single_term_2_order_by_card on full, a nested terms on 100 segments) keep an 11 % to 15 % spread over three runs; they are either very short (0.6 ms) or dominated by cross-segment merging. The report uses the median of the three runs. A run of the previous version that was accidentally made with the v2 binary while v3 was expected doubled as a fourth v2 run, and confirmed the columnar times within 1 %.
